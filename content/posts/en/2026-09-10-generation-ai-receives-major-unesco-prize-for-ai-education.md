Generation AI is one of two recipients of the 2026 UNESCO King Hamad Bin Isa Al-Khalifa Prize for the Use of Information and Communication Technologies (ICTs) in Education. The other laureate is the Redação Paraná programme from the State of Paraná, Brazil.

The recipients were selected from more than one hundred nominations submitted by UNESCO Member States and partner organisations. The prize was presented on 9 September at UNESCO headquarters in Paris as part of the Digital Learning Week event, held this year under the theme *Education in the age of AI: Facts | Frictions | Frontiers*.

UNESCO's international expert jury drew particular attention to the strength of the evidence behind Generation AI. The project develops open tools, learning materials and pedagogical models that help learners move beyond passive use of AI to investigate, evaluate, build and reshape AI systems themselves.

In introducing the laureates, UNESCO highlighted the project's international reach and evidence base:

> "Generation AI gives learners free open source tools to build, question and redesign AI systems, moving them beyond passive AI use. Its resources have been accessed over 200,000 times across more than 50 countries, demonstrating the scalability of the initiative beyond the Finnish borders. What particularly impressed the jury was the strength of the evidence behind Generation AI, with numerous peer-reviewed studies demonstrating sustained and rigorous scientific research into critical thinking and creativity with AI, and also providing an important exemplar of evidence-led approaches to utilizing AI in education."

## AI literacy is for everyone

The prize was received by the leader of Generation AI, Professor Matti Tedre, and University Researcher Henriikka Vartiainen from the University of Eastern Finland.

In his acceptance speech, Tedre highlighted the impact of AI on everyday life, decision-making, communication and how knowledge is created. AI is used much more widely than the most visible generative AI applications suggest, yet most people still know very little about how these systems actually work.

> "If AI is changing how we live, how we decide, and what we know, then shouldn't everyone understand how it works, where it's used, and all that?" Tedre asked in his speech.

According to Tedre, the societal impact of AI is too significant for its direction to be shaped by only a few. Meaningful participation, however, requires that people have the knowledge and capacity to understand AI systems and their effects.

The speech gave particular thanks to the teachers, children and the project's research team who have contributed to the long-term co-development of the tools and materials.

## Beyond using AI – assessing and shaping it

In her remarks, Henriikka Vartiainen described the pedagogical foundations of Generation AI. Its starting points are equity, social justice and children's right to learn AI literacies.

> "It is not just about how AI systems are used, but also about how they are critically assessed, shaped and made," Vartiainen said.

Generation AI's pedagogical models and educational technologies have been developed through interdisciplinary research together with hundreds of teachers, pre-service teachers and children. According to Vartiainen, policy decisions concerning education should be based on research rather than opinion.

The project's aim is not only to help people adapt to technological change. Its purpose is to strengthen transformative data agency: people's capacity to examine AI scientifically, critically and ethically, to question existing practices and to imagine and build alternatives.

> "Education shouldn't be about adapting to technological change, but cultivate transformative data agency," Vartiainen emphasised.

## Research-based tools in more than 50 countries

Generation AI is a multidisciplinary research programme developed by the University of Eastern Finland, the University of Helsinki and the University of Oulu together with other partners. It produces research-based pedagogical models, learning materials and open tools for exploring how AI works and how it affects society.

According to UNESCO, the project's materials and tools have been accessed more than 200,000 times in over 50 countries. Around 1,500 students and teachers have taken part in the project's workshops and school-based interventions.

Generation AI's tools allow learners to build classifiers based on machine learning, examine social media profiling and recommendation algorithms, investigate biases in AI systems and train their own small language model. The tools are designed to open up the workings of AI in practice without requiring prior programming skills.

## About the prize

The UNESCO King Hamad Bin Isa Al-Khalifa Prize for the Use of Information and Communication Technologies in Education has been awarded annually since 2005 and is supported by the Kingdom of Bahrain. Each year it highlights projects and approaches that use digital technology responsibly to enhance teaching and learning.

The 2026 edition focused on uses of AI that support learners' critical thinking, creativity and imagination.
